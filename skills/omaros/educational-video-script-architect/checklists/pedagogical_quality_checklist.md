# Pedagogical Quality Checklist

Run during §11 of the output. For each item: **PASS / NOTE / FAIL**, with a short explanation.

## Motivation and necessity

- [ ] Every definition is preceded by the conceptual pressure that motivates it. The viewer would *invent* the definition (or want it to exist) before it appears.
- [ ] No definition arrives by fiat. No "let X be Y because that's the definition" without prior justification.
- [ ] The video opens with a question, problem, phenomenon, or tension — not with a label.

## Audience adaptation

- [ ] The audience level is explicit and consistent across the video. No sudden jumps in prerequisite knowledge.
- [ ] Notation density matches the audience tier (see `SKILL.md §4`).
- [ ] Pace matches the audience tier — beginner-tier video does not race; research-tier does not over-explain.
- [ ] Prerequisites listed in the production brief are honest (not aspirational, not bloated).

## Misconception management

- [ ] At least one likely misconception is named explicitly and addressed.
- [ ] If a metaphor is used, its limits are stated (in narration or in production notes).
- [ ] No "false friends" — informal language that the viewer might mistake for formal claims is flagged or avoided.

## Cognitive load

- [ ] No scene introduces more than one new abstract object at a time without giving the viewer time to process.
- [ ] Definitions, examples, and abstractions are chunked, not piled.
- [ ] Redundant cues (visual + verbal pointing to the same thing) are used at high-cognitive-load moments.
- [ ] Complex constructions are built incrementally on screen, not revealed all at once.

## Sequencing logic

- [ ] The pedagogy mode (example-first / definition-first / proof-first / problem-first / phenomenon-first) is declared and applied consistently.
- [ ] Examples genuinely instantiate the definition that follows them (or precedes them). No bait-and-switch.
- [ ] Special cases are revealed only after the general case has the viewer's attention, OR they motivate the general case explicitly.
- [ ] The "pill of abstraction" arrives at a moment when the viewer has enough examples to recognize the pattern, not before.

## Visual-narrative synchronization

- [ ] No voice-over describes an object that is not yet on screen (or has just left).
- [ ] No on-screen object is left unexplained for more than 2 s when it carries new information.
- [ ] The narration and the visual event reinforce each other; neither is mere decoration of the other.

## Conceptual transformation

- [ ] At the end, the viewer can state what they understand now that they did not before. (Write the sentence in §2.)
- [ ] The closing scene returns to the opening question or phenomenon and resolves it explicitly.
- [ ] The video does not end on a definition or a formula; it ends on a re-encounter with the original question, now clearer.

## Style and presence

- [ ] No banned phrases (see `style/narration_style_guide.md`).
- [ ] No filler (no "as you can see", "essentially", "basically", "kind of", "sort of").
- [ ] No motivational fluff disguised as pedagogy.
- [ ] The narration sounds like an intelligent person speaking with care, not like a tutorial template.

## Learning-research influence (only if `learning_research/` is populated)

- [ ] At least one principle from the folder is cited briefly in §3.
- [ ] The cited principle visibly shapes the design (not just name-dropped).
- [ ] No principle was applied in a way that softened mathematical correctness.
