# Core claim

Comparative review of six leading models of self-regulated learning (SRL) — Zimmerman, Pintrich, Boekaerts, Winne & Hadwin, Efklides, Hadwin et al. All share a cyclical structure: planning / forethought → monitoring / control → reflection / evaluation. Differences are in granularity and which loops are emphasised.

# What this paper is useful for in educational video design

A taxonomy of *what good learners do between viewings*. A video can scaffold each phase: pre-viewing (set a goal), during-viewing (monitor understanding), post-viewing (reflect on what was learned).

# Actionable principles

1. Optionally prompt the viewer at the *start* of the video to set a learning goal ("What do you want to be able to do after this?").
2. During the video, prompts are not just retrieval — they are also *monitoring* ("Do you understand why this step works?"), which is metacognitive.
3. End the video with an explicit reflection prompt rather than a summary that does the reflection for the viewer.

# How the video-script skill should use this

Use as the secondary justification (after retrieval-practice and active-learning) for engineering viewer-side prompts. Frame these prompts as *metacognitive* moments, not as quizzes.

# Risks / limitations / caveats

SRL frameworks are descriptive, not strongly prescriptive. The models agree on the cycle but differ on the details — do not over-cite a single model. Goal-setting prompts can feel didactic in short-form videos; use sparingly.
