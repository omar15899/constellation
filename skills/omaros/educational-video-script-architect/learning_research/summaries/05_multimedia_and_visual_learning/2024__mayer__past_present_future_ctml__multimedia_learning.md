# Core claim

Mayer's recent retrospective on CTML: which principles have held up, where the boundary conditions are clearer, and how the theory needs to extend to interactive media, immersive environments, and AI-generated content. Emphasises *generative* processing principles (self-explanation, drawing, summarising) alongside the older *attention-management* principles.

# What this paper is useful for in educational video design

Brings the design principles into the era of generative video and AI tutoring. Especially useful when the script proposes a hybrid pipeline that mixes Manim, generative video, and learner-side prompts.

# Actionable principles

1. The classical CTML principles still hold under modern conditions.
2. Generative-processing principles (asking the viewer to predict, explain, summarise, draw) are now first-class design moves, not optional add-ons.
3. Personalisation, voice, and embodiment principles are weaker than once thought; do not over-invest in conversational style.

# How the video-script skill should use this

Use to justify the inclusion of generative prompts (predict, explain, classify) inside the second-by-second script. Use also to push back against over-styled "chatty" narration.

# Risks / limitations / caveats

The 2024 retrospective is partly self-promotional; some weak principles are presented more favourably than the meta-analytic evidence supports. Treat principle-strength claims with calibrated skepticism.

# Keywords

CTML; generative processing; personalisation; multimedia learning; recent synthesis.
