# Core claim

Direct experimental demonstration that *prompting* learners to self-explain improves learning even for those who would not have done so spontaneously. Eighth-graders who were prompted to explain text about the circulatory system gained twice as much as a control group.

# What this paper is useful for in educational video design

Justifies *engineering* the self-explanation moments rather than hoping for them. A video can prompt the viewer; it does not have to wait for spontaneous metacognition.

# Actionable principles

1. Use explicit prompts ("Pause for a moment — why does this step work?") rather than implicit invitations.
2. Even prompts that go unanswered have effect — the cognitive set is what matters.
3. Embed prompts at moments of conceptual transition, not at trivia points.

# How the video-script skill should use this

When the script reaches a non-obvious move (substitution, choice of technique, lemma), a self-explanation prompt is preferable to immediate narration of the reason.

# Risks / limitations / caveats

Too many prompts trivialise the device. Use sparingly, at moments of genuine non-obviousness.
