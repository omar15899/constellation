# Core claim

Showed that *interleaving* paintings by different artists during study leads to better artist-classification of *new* paintings than blocking by artist — even though learners *believed* blocking was more effective. The spacing-as-interleaving manipulation improves *inductive* learning, not just rote recall.

# What this paper is useful for in educational video design

Provides the empirical case for using interleaved examples when the goal is conceptual *discrimination* — learning to tell similar concepts apart. Crucial for any video about classification, definition boundaries, or competing models.

# Actionable principles

1. When teaching to discriminate (e.g., type-I vs type-II error, Itô vs Stratonovich, push-forward vs pull-back), interleave examples of the alternatives rather than presenting them in pure blocks.
2. The viewer's subjective sense of difficulty is a poor guide to actual learning — they will *prefer* blocked exposure even when it is worse.
3. Counter-intuitive results often warrant explicit narration ("this feels like jumping around — that is the point").

# How the video-script skill should use this

When the central pedagogical task is discrimination, the script should interleave example types (A, B, A, B, A) rather than block them (A, A, A, B, B, B). Mention the design choice in pedagogical-design notes.

# Risks / limitations / caveats

Interleaving raises cognitive load on first encounter; novices may need a single anchor example before interleaving begins. Interleaving works for discrimination, not for first introduction of a single concept — for which blocked massed practice may be better.
