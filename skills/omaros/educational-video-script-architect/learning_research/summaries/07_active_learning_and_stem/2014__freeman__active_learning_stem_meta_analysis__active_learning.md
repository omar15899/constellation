# Core claim

Meta-analysis of 225 STEM-classroom studies. Active learning raises mean exam performance by ~6% and reduces failure rates by ~55% (from 34% to 22%) compared to traditional lecture. Effects hold across class size, course type, and STEM discipline.

# What this paper is useful for in educational video design

Establishes that *passive exposure is the worst case*. The video equivalent of "traditional lecture" is uninterrupted narration over slides; the video equivalent of "active learning" is a script that asks the viewer to do something — predict, classify, explain, compare.

# Actionable principles

1. A video that asks nothing of the viewer is the analog of a failing lecture format.
2. The active intervention need not be elaborate; a 5-second prediction prompt is enough to shift the cognitive mode.
3. Effects are large enough that *not* including active prompts is a meaningful design failure.

# How the video-script skill should use this

Treat the absence of viewer-engagement prompts as a quality-control failure, not as a stylistic choice. The QC checklist already enforces this; cite this paper when justifying.

# Risks / limitations / caveats

The meta-analysis is on classrooms, not videos. Inference is plausible — the cognitive mode is similar — but the evidence is indirect. The effect size for short-form educational videos with embedded prompts is empirically supported but smaller and less consistent than for full courses.
