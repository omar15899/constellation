# Core claim

The mature synthesis of CLT. Catalogues seven instructional design effects: goal-free, worked-example, completion-problem, split-attention, modality, redundancy, and variability. Each prescribes specific design moves that exploit the architecture of human working memory.

# What this paper is useful for in educational video design

A direct catalogue of design moves with empirical backing. The *modality* effect (audio narration + visual diagram outperforms on-screen-text + visual diagram) and the *redundancy* effect (audio narration + identical on-screen text *underperforms* either alone) are the most directly relevant to video.

# Actionable principles

1. **Modality**: prefer voice-over + visuals over on-screen text + visuals.
2. **Redundancy**: do not duplicate the voice-over verbatim on screen — choose one channel per piece of information.
3. **Split-attention**: place labels next to the object they label, not in a legend.
4. **Variability**: when teaching a concept, vary surface features across examples while preserving deep structure.

# How the video-script skill should use this

These four effects should be enforced by the visual-quality checklist. They directly determine whether the on-screen text in the second-by-second script is permissible or wasteful.

# Risks / limitations / caveats

The redundancy effect reverses for low-knowledge learners under noisy listening conditions: brief on-screen redundancy can help when the audio is the only channel and conditions are degraded. In a controlled video studio, the rule holds.
