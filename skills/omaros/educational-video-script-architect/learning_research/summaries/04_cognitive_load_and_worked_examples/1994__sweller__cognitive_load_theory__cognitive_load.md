# Core claim

Foundational statement of *cognitive load theory* (CLT). Working memory has narrow capacity; learning consists of building schemas in long-term memory; instruction succeeds when it minimises *extraneous* load and maximises *germane* load while respecting the *intrinsic* load of the material.

# What this paper is useful for in educational video design

The theoretical backbone for every design decision about visual density, narration overlap, on-screen text, and pacing. Without CLT, decisions about "how much is too much on screen" are arbitrary.

# Actionable principles

1. **Reduce extraneous load**: do not narrate text that is also on screen verbatim; do not split attention between distant visual elements that must be integrated; do not decorate.
2. **Respect intrinsic load**: high-element-interactivity material (e.g., a definition with three quantifiers and two function spaces) cannot be sped up without loss.
3. **Encourage germane load**: guide attention to the structure being learned, not to surface features.

# How the video-script skill should use this

Every scene's visual-density check (`visual_quality_checklist.md`) is downstream of CLT. The split-attention rule (do not narrate text identical to on-screen text) is the most often-violated.

# Risks / limitations / caveats

Element-interactivity is hard to measure in advance. CLT predicts *averaged* effects; individual learners with high prior knowledge can absorb more — see Kalyuga et al. (2003) on expertise reversal.
